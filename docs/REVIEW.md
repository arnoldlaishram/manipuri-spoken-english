# Review: could she actually learn from this?

Read as a teacher, and as someone who reads **only romanized Manipuri**.
Every screen was opened and read, not assumed. Dated 2026-09-19.

**Short answer: yes for the first three levels, thinning badly after that.**
The mechanics work. The teaching is sound. The content runs out long before
she does, and one whole skill is missing.

---

## What she can actually do after finishing it

Honestly, and only counting what the app genuinely trains:

| She can | Confidence |
|---|---|
| Say ~110 fixed English sentences intelligibly | **High** — drilled, spaced, spoken |
| Escape any conversation she is lost in | **High** — that is Level 1, by design |
| Build a new sentence in a pattern she has met | **Medium** — drilled, but from few models |
| Understand a stranger speaking at normal speed | **Low** — barely trained at all |
| Hold an unscripted two-minute conversation | **Low** — only with a key, only in role-play |

That is a solid **A1**. It is not A2, and the app does not claim to be.

---

## Where a Manipuri-only reader is fine

- **Home.** Every level and every step now carries a Manipuri line under the
  English. She can pick where to go without reading English.
- **The pattern screen.** The explanation is given in both, English above,
  Manipuri under, with a play button.
- **Examples.** Six English sentences, each with its meaning under it and a
  play button. Nothing is asked of her. This is the strongest screen in the app.
- **Drills and scenes.** English to say, Manipuri meaning under it, instructions
  in Manipuri.
- **Failure states.** Microphone problems, no-storage, no-key — all explained in
  Manipuri first.

## Where she is still stranded

1. **The role-play is English-only, by design.** The other speaker's lines have
   no translation. That is correct for a conversation simulator and wrong for a
   beginner who loses the thread — she has the escape phrases, but no way back
   in. *Not yet fixed.*
2. **The assessment tells her a number, not what to fix.** "4/6" and a nudge
   back to the examples. It does not say *which* sentences she missed. *Not yet
   fixed.*
3. **The header tag is English-only** (`WORD ORDER — THE ONE BIG DIFFERENCE`).
   Cosmetic, but it is the first thing on the screen.

---

## Teaching judgement, level by level

**Level 1 — Houba.** Right. Escape phrases before anything else means she can
never be trapped, which is the thing that stops beginners speaking at all. Word
order second is the correct first grammar point for a Manipuri speaker.

**Level 2 — Yumda.** Right. Present tense plus the safest possible room. She
practises on a scene where the stakes are zero.

**Level 3 — Marup amasung dukan.** Prepositions here is correct — it is the
second structural difference after word order, and it was the thing originally
asked for. Articles alongside it is a lot of abstraction in one level; this is
the level I would most expect her to stall on.

**Level 4 — Ngarang.** The tense table is the best teaching object in the app.
One action down five tenses, side by side, is worth more than any explanation.
**It is also the least verified Manipuri in the app** — the verb endings were
guessed. If they are wrong, this level teaches confident nonsense.

**Levels 5–8.** Thin. Two lessons and some scenes each. By Level 6 she is being
asked to describe things and talk about frequency having met each pattern six
times. That is not enough repetition to own it.

---

## The three things that would most improve learning

1. **Listening.** She has no connected speech to understand — only isolated
   phrases. It is a quarter of a balanced course and it is at zero. Everything
   else is polish next to this.
2. **More models per pattern.** Six examples and six drills per lesson is thin
   for someone starting from nothing. Twelve of each would roughly double the
   value of what is already built, at no structural cost.
3. **Tell her what she got wrong.** The assessment scores but does not teach.
   Listing the missed sentences with a play button would turn it from a gate
   into a lesson.

---

## The risk that outranks all of the above

**Most of the Manipuri has never been checked.** Three errors have been found by
a native speaker so far, every one of them in ordinary vocabulary a learner
would trust. The tense table is guessed verb morphology.

She cannot tell a wrong explanation from a right one. That is the difference
between this helping her and teaching her mistakes with confidence.
